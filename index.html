Seamline

Finds where two utilities' planned construction comes within 40 km, ranks each match by how close it is and whether it's built in the same window, and estimates what coordinating would save.

Add a utility's plans

Drop the plan the way the utility published it: an Excel project list, a CSV, or GIS files (KML/KMZ, shapefile as a .zip or the .shp, .dbf and .prj together, GeoJSON). Column names are matched loosely, so owner, voltage, isd and latitude all work, and title rows above a spreadsheet's header are skipped. Tables need coordinates for each project; GIS files carry their own. Year-only dates like 2029 are fine.

    Built-in data. DESC: SCRTP 2026 to 2030 project descriptions ($2M and above). Georgia: SERTP 2026 preliminary expansion plan, 2025 SERTP report, and Georgia Power's pages for Callaway Road to Thomson and Effingham County. Georgia's grid is planned jointly by Georgia Power, Georgia Transmission and MEAG, so all three appear as one system.
    
    
    Method. Distance is measured between the closest points of two projects, not their centers. Pairs within 40 km are flagged and tiered: touching, under 1.6 km, under 8 km, under 40 km. Ranking is tier first, then whether the build windows overlap, then distance. Plans list in-service dates, so construction start is estimated from type, voltage and length unless a start date is given.
    
    
    Assumptions. Public plans name substations, not coordinates, so built-in locations are placed by hand; dashed lines are best guesses. Grey markers are existing assets for reference. Cost model: the plan's cost where listed; otherwise about $2.5M/km for new 500 kV line, $1.4M/km at 230 kV, $0.95M/km at 115 kV, half that for rebuilds, and $60M, $25M or $8M for 500, 230 or 115 kV substations. Savings are half of a 5% mobilization cost (same window, under 40 km), one $400K laydown yard (under 8 km), shared right-of-way and permits (under 1.6 km) and one $250K outage (touching).
